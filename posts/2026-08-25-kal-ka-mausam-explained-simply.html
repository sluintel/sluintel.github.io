The reported temperature is often just one piece of the puzzle. You need to consider the expected highs and lows for the day and night. Crucially, pay attention to the "feels like" temperature, which accounts for wind chill or humidity. A 25°C day with high humidity can feel far hotter than a dry 28°C day. Precipitation Probability: Beyond the Percentage

A "30% chance of rain" doesn't mean it will only rain for 30% of the day. It typically means there's a 30% chance that any rain will occur in the forecast area during a specific period. For critical plans, anything above 50% should prompt a contingency. Always check the expected rainfall amount and its duration for better preparedness.

Wind Speed and Direction: A Game Changer for Comfort

Wind can dramatically alter how a temperature feels. A strong breeze on a cool day will make it much colder, while on a hot day, it can offer welcome relief. Wind direction also matters, indicating whether the air is coming from a cooler or warmer region. For activities like cycling or flying kites, this data is non-negotiable.

Humidity and Dew Point: The Invisible Comfort Factor
