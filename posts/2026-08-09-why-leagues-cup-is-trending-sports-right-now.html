The Leagues Cup is an annual football tournament sanctioned by CONCACAF, featuring all clubs from Major League Soccer (MLS) in the United States and Canada, and Liga MX in Mexico. This inter-league showdown offers genuine bragging rights and, crucially, direct qualification spots for the CONCACAF Champions Cup, making it a pivotal event in the regional football calendar.

Navigating the Tournament: How Leagues Cup Works

Understanding the structure of the Leagues Cup helps appreciate its intensity. It's designed to maximize drama and direct competition between MLS and Liga MX teams.

  1. Group Stage (Early Season): The tournament kicks off with a group stage featuring teams divided into regions. Each group typically consists of three teams (one from Liga MX, two from MLS, or vice-versa, with some top teams receiving byes). Teams play each other once.
  2. Points System: A win earns 3 points, a draw leads to a penalty shootout where the winner gets 2 points and the loser 1 point. This ensures every match has a clear outcome and keeps teams fighting for every possible point.
  3. Advancing to Knockout: The top two teams from each group advance to the knockout rounds.
  4. Knockout Rounds (Round of 32 to Final): From the Round of 32 onwards, it's a single-elimination bracket. Overtime isn't typically played; if scores are level after 90 minutes, matches go straight to a penalty shootout. This increases the tension and stakes of every game.
  5. CONCACAF Champions Cup Qualification: The champion, runner-up, and third-place team all earn berths in the CONCACAF Champions Cup, making the tournament incredibly important for teams aspiring to continental glory. This direct pathway significantly elevates its importance compared to many other regional cups.
