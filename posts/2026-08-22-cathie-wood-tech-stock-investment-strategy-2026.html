  1. Identify the Core Innovation: What specific technological breakthrough or new business model is at play? How significant is its potential to change an industry?

  2. Understand the Ecosystem: No innovation exists in a vacuum. Who are the enablers (e.g., chip manufacturers for AI)? Who are the potential beneficiaries? Consider the entire value chain.

  3. Evaluate Total Addressable Market (TAM): Don't just look at the current market; project what the market could become if the innovation achieves widespread adoption. ARK often looks for 10x or greater potential. This requires significant imagination and conviction.

  4. Assess the Company's Moat: Does the company have a sustainable competitive advantage? Is it network effects, proprietary technology, strong brand, or regulatory barriers? Strong intellectual property (IP) is often crucial.

  5. Scrutinize Management & Culture: Are the leaders visionary? Do they have a culture of innovation? Disruption often comes from unconventional thinkers who are willing to challenge the status quo.

  6. Consider Valuation with a Long-Term Lens: Disruptive innovators often trade at high valuations based on future potential. Focus less on near-term P/E ratios and more on discounted future cash flows or market share potential over a 5-10 year horizon. This means being comfortable with short-term volatility.
